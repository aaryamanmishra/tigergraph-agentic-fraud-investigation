## Post 2: LinkedIn

> **From Suspicious Signal to Defensible Action: Introducing FraudNet**
> 
> In modern financial institutions, risk models flag transactions, but human analysts spend hours manually pivoting across systems to uncover connected infrastructure, evaluate card velocity, and determine regulatory filing requirements.
> 
> For the **Hacker House Goa 2026 Challenge**, we built **FraudNet** — an autonomous, graph-native fraud investigation platform.
> 
> **How FraudNet Works:**
> 1. **Trigger Ingestion:** Treats transaction risk scores not as verdicts, but as investigation triggers.
> 2. **TigerGraph & MCP:** Connects directly via the official `tigergraph-mcp` interface to run parameterized GSQL queries, uncovering hidden syndicate infrastructure (like 34 cards sharing a single device profile in Case HHG-014).
> 3. **GraphRAG Case Memory:** Pulls context from 5,565 historical closed cases and bank typologies before reasoning begins.
> 4. **Uncertainty & Evidence Loops:** Refuses to make unsupported blocks on weak signals; requests additional evidence (e.g. customer verification) to clear false alarms (like routine travel in Case HHG-001).
> 5. **Deterministic Policy Enforcement:** Ensures actions, approval routes (AUTO/L1/L2), and FinCEN SAR filings are legally authoritative and explainable.
> 6. **Case Memory Writeback:** Closes investigations by persisting findings back to TigerGraph for future organizational memory.
> 
> The system was evaluated across all 20 benchmark exam cases with 100% output schema validation and entity grounding across 631,000+ dataset entities.
